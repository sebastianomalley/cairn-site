The basics

What a doula is, and isn't

What I do

  • Talk through the big stuff, the small stuff and what nobody else wants to bring up
  • Help you get your wishes written down and into the right hands
  • Plan the vigil, the music, light and who's in the room
  • Give your family a break so they can rest and just be family
  • Stay through the last days and the hours after
  • Help with letters, recorded stories and legacy projects
  • Check in with the family in the weeks that follow

What I don't do

  • Medical care of any kind. That's your doctor and hospice team.
  • Legal advice. I'll point you to the right forms and people.
  • Replace hospice. I work alongside them.
  • Tell you how to feel, what to believe or how to grieve
  • Share your story with anyone without your okay

Fair questions

Questions people ask me

Isn't that what hospice does?

Hospice is wonderful and takes care of the medical side. I work alongside them on the emotional and practical side, and I can spend the kind of long, unhurried time with you that a care team often can't.

Do I have to be sick to call you?

Not at all. Some of the best planning happens years ahead, over coffee, with no rush at all.

Isn't it all really sad?

There are tears, sure. There's also a surprising amount of laughter, great stories and the occasional debate about the playlist.

Do I need to be religious?

Nope. I meet you where you are, whatever you believe or don't.

Do you help with pets?

Yes. I'm also certified to help families say goodbye to a beloved companion animal.

Is what I tell you private?

Yes. What you share with me stays with me. I don't share your story, photos or film with anyone without your written okay.

Does insurance cover this?

Not usually. Doula care is typically paid privately. We'll talk openly about cost on our first call.
